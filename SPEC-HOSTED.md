# Hosted Measurement Protocol: Managed Inference Endpoints
### Project: Percentes. This document is the pre-registered protocol SPEC.md §6 requires before any hosted-provider data is collected.
### Status: drafted 2026-08-19, revised 2026-09-26. Hosted amendments 1 and 3 (HA1 and HA3) adopted 2026-10-19, with HA3 modifying HA1 where they differ, and written into §1 to §9. Pre-data: no provider data has been collected under this document, and no release has published.

## Read this first

The normative text starts at §0; this part adds nothing to it and changes nothing in it.

**What is measured.** One large language model (LLM) behind one provider's hosted application programming interface (API), reached from one client machine through one ordinary paid account. The protocol calls that combination a route. For every request it sends to a route it records which of three states the request ended in: completed (split into within the 30 s deadline and late), errored (failed, with the failure classed), or still unanswered at 90 s, which the protocol calls censored. A scheduled request a stop left unsent is counted on its own line. It also records how long each completion took to start and to finish.

**Against which providers.** This document carries no list of providers. A release's routes are the routes whose configuration files are frozen, with their recorded limits, before its collection window opens, and its dated page names each one. Where a rule depends on one provider's own documentation, the rule names that provider (§1, §3 and §9); those names attach the rule and do not list a release's routes. A provider whose terms do not permit the planned traffic and the publication is not measured (§2, terms review); where its terms permit publication only with its written permission, its route enters only with that permission in hand. Each route must use one of two recognised streaming request shapes: the chat-completions shape, whose stream the instrument reads to its `data: [DONE]` frame, or the Anthropic Messages shape, whose stream ends at `message_stop`. A provider with another shape is measured only after a numbered amendment adds its shape.

**How.** The client sends requests on a schedule fixed in advance and never retries. That schedule is open loop: a slow answer never delays the next send. One cell is one run of a route's ladder, 48.5 minutes when all five steps are admitted: a 60 s warm-up, then five steps of offered load at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 requests per second, 480 s each, with a 90 s drain after every step in which nothing is sent, so every request of a step has ended before the next step starts. Twelve cells per route cover six Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) start times, each once on a weekday and once on a weekend day.

Before a route runs, three kinds of limit are recorded: every limit the provider publishes for the account, every value its console shows, and every limit the operator set. A step is admitted only where its nominal demand (the requests, tokens and spend the step asks for, counted at the worst case per request) is at most half of each rate, token, concurrency, daily and spend-window limit, and where the monthly and liability caps cover the route's worst case over its twelve cells, two re-runs and 100 probe requests. On a prepaid account that tops itself up automatically, the balance at which it tops up must be at least the worst-case cost of the route's costliest cell. That is the ceiling rule. A limit the provider documents without a published value caps the route at 0.5 requests per second or excludes it. A step that fails a test is excluded, and the page names the limit that excluded it.

The request body is one fixed JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) document per route; the only part that changes per request is the user text: a unique prefix `cs-<seed>-<index>`, a space and a synthetic support ticket. Before the window, a ten-request check per route chooses the output ceiling for the model, 512 tokens or the 1,024-token fallback. At each cell's start the route's limits and model documentation pages are read again; a cell whose records were not refreshed does not start, and a route whose limits as read no longer admit one of its steps stops. At each cell's start the route's balance command also reads the account's balance; it blocks the cell when a readable balance is below the cell's reservation (its probe and scheduled requests at the ledger's price), when the command fails, or when the balance is unavailable and the route records no `balance_unavailable` ruling (§3).

**What publishes, and what does not.** Published per cell: the configuration file, one record per request (`attempts.jsonl`), the probe, clock, status, refresh and balance records, the dispatch series, the ceiling-check records of every route serving the same model, the report pair (the machine-readable report and its text rendering), and a manifest carrying the instrument commit and the SHA-256 (256-bit Secure Hash Algorithm) hash of every file, except that on a route with a confidential limit it names the frame store and the operator records by their keyed hash (a hash under a secret key the operator keeps, §9); per route, its page and its window-close record (its limits and model documentation read once more after its last cell); per release, the index, and on the page the dates and outcomes the publication record holds. The route page shows, per step and per cell, each outcome as a count over the step's scheduled requests with a 95 percent interval; each step pooled over the cells that attempted it; the completion curve with its bands; and the time to first token and whole-answer distributions. One index table across routes carries counts only, in alphabetical order, with no ranking. Not published: the model's answers (kept 12 months, unpublished, where the route's recorded terms permit keeping them, and otherwise not kept at all), response header values a provider's terms keep confidential, the value of a limit that only the account console shows under terms that keep it confidential (its source and the keyed hash of its retained snapshot publish in its place; a limit taken from a public documentation page publishes in full), request identifiers where terms restrict them, and the legal name on the account, unless the operator's dated record publishes it. The page carries no quality score and no price comparison, and it estimates no provider-wide reliability: it describes the dated runs it reports.

**Fixed in advance.** This document, with every amendment its log lists as adopted, is public in this repository before a release sends its first paid request, which is a ceiling-check request. Before the collection window opens, the following are committed here: the master seed and the schedule it derives (each cell's date and slot, and each route's offset); each route's configuration file as published (§9), with its request body frozen by hash; the recorded limits with their hashes; the ceiling each model runs at, with the check records of every host of the model that chose it; and the admitted steps. Changing a date and slot after the window opens is a numbered amendment (§4), and a change of limits or model documentation inside the window splits the pooled figures and changes no admitted step, while limits that no longer admit a step the route runs stop the route (§4, drift). These are fixed before any cell runs, so no rate, step, date or ceiling is chosen after a cell's result is seen.

**How to read a result.** A per-cell line on a route page prints as `count/scheduled rate [low, high]`: a count over the step's scheduled requests, the rate, and a 95 percent Wilson interval. The Wilson interval is the range of rates consistent with the count. A pooled line adds the range of cell rates, the cells with events, the design effect (DEFF) and the effective sample size its interval uses. A rate pooled over several cells prints a wider interval when its events bunch in a few cells, because requests inside one cell share its hour and conditions. Example, twelve cells of 120 requests at one step: one event in each cell gives a DEFF of 1.00 and an interval of 0.48 to 1.45 percent; all twelve events in one cell give a DEFF of 12.10, an effective sample of 119 requests, and an interval of 0.15 to 4.60 percent. §8 gives the formula. The index sets two routes' counts side by side only at the same step, and the protocol makes no ranked claim between routes (§1). SPEC.md §11 bars winner language until a comparison has a measured noise floor (how much the same measurement varies from run to run) and a pre-registered minimum detectable effect (the smallest difference the comparison is built to detect), and a release has neither. A test fails the build if the index carries any of the strings rank, winner, best, worst, interval, sort key or p99 (the 99th percentile). A step marked "excluded, binding limit X" was never sent: its nominal demand failed the test against limit X: at most half of X for a rate, token, concurrency, daily or spend-window limit, at most X for a cap, and at most 0.5 requests per second for a limit the provider documents without a value. Where X is a confidential limit, the line names its source and hash in place of its value. A zero count gives an upper bound: 0 events in the 120 requests expected at the lowest step leave an upper bound of 3.10 percent. Two checks apply to every cell whose ladder runs. They carry the numbers of SPEC.md §10's run-validity gates, G1 to G7, of which only these two can be evaluated against a hosted endpoint. G2, the client-validity gate, withholds a cell and has it re-run when the client machine was the bottleneck: requests sent late (send skew, the time from intended to actual send, above 5 ms at the 99th percentile or above 50 ms for any request), a scheduled request never sent, central processing unit (CPU) use above 70 percent over any 5 s window, or a garbage-collection pause 99th percentile of 1 ms or more. Hosted-G6 is the fraction of scheduled requests completed within the 30 s deadline, printed against a 0.99 line; it never withholds a cell.

## 1. The measurement

One measurement covers one route at each step of the pinned ladder, and a release sets several routes' counts side by side in one unranked table.

**Question:** for one model on one managed endpoint, at each step of the ladder, what fraction of scheduled requests complete within the 30 s deadline, and what are the client-observed distributions of time to first token (TTFT), end-to-end latency, and completion length, sampled across a fixed grid of times of day and day types?

**Target:** one provider, one model identifier, one endpoint, one account tier per run. The complete request URL (uniform resource locator) is configured per target and recorded; the harness appends nothing. Two streaming request shapes are recognised: the OpenAI chat-completions shape (a POST whose Server-Sent Events (SSE) frames end at `data: [DONE]`) and the Anthropic Messages shape (a POST whose event-typed SSE frames end at `message_stop`). A target whose documented streaming path is neither is not characterized until a numbered amendment adds its shape. Google's OpenAI-compatible path is a chat-completions target, and every Gemini index row names it.

**Fault variant:** `none`. The harness has no authority over a provider's infrastructure, and no fault is injected.

**Scope of a release:** a set of route characterizations under §1 to §9, published together on one dated page with the index below. A route is one provider, one model identifier, one endpoint and one account tier. A characterization enters a release only with every artifact of §9 and the publication steps of §9.

**Comparison scope:** a release publishes one index table across its routes, alphabetical by provider, one row per route and offered-load step, with identical count fields and the denominator shown, a step a route did not admit marked with its binding limit, and a link to each route's page. The index carries no rate, no interval, no sort by a measured value and no ranking or winner language. The pointwise interval bands SPEC.md §3 requires are published on each route's page. Any ranked claim stays barred until SPEC.md §11's conditions are met. A report test fails the build if the index carries a rank, winner, best, worst, interval, sort key or p99 (99th percentile) string.

## 2. Endpoint, model, and client host

Every record in the table is taken before a route's first run; the ones marked per cell are taken again at each cell's start. The bullets below the table are the rule.

| Record | What it fixes | When |
|---|---|---|
| Model identifier | the exact string sent, and whether the provider documents it as one fixed build | before the first run; per run |
| Reported identifier | the model name each response carries, against the expected one | every response |
| Response shape and ceiling | which field carries the answer, which carries reasoning, where usage and any refusal field appear, the finish reasons seen, and the ceiling the model runs at | the ten-request ceiling check before the first run |
| Endpoint and account | the complete request URL, the provider's declared region, the account tier, the published status at start and end | per cell |
| Account | one account, organisation and key per provider; its type, tier, creation date and pre-window traffic | before the first run |
| Terms review | automated access, publication of results, republishing response metadata, keeping model output | before the first run |
| Dedicated account | the account and key serve no other workload during the window | per cell |
| Documentation snapshots | a retained copy and SHA-256 hash of every page a record relies on; the limits and model documentation pages re-read at each cell's start | every documentation record; per cell |
| Client host | one dedicated non-spot virtual machine, named, with its open-file limit, vendor affiliation and round-trip time | per characterization; per cell |
| Credentials | the environment variable's name only; the key is never written | per cell |

- **Exact model identifier, recorded, no aliases.** The identifier sent on the wire (`target.model_name`) is recorded per run. An identifier the provider's published documentation describes as a pointer that can resolve to different underlying builds over time is ineligible, because the measurement would not name what it measured. Eligibility is checked against the provider's model documentation and recorded per run with the documentation URL and the retrieval date. Where the documentation does not state whether the identifier is fixed to one build, the identifier is recorded as stability-not-documented and the characterization publishes with that label.
- **Reported identifier.** Where a response carries a model identifier, it is recorded and compared with the expected one, per response. The expected identifier is the requested one, unless the ceiling check below returns a different string and the provider's model documentation, recorded with URL and retrieval date, states that both names refer to one fixed build; the check's string is then the expected value, recorded in the route's configuration with that documentation. Where the check's string differs and the documentation is silent, the provider-model is recorded as identifier-not-documented and is not characterized. A host is also kept out where its check reports an identifier other than the documented one, or none where one is documented. Two check records of one host that expect different identifiers keep it out too. A host this rule keeps out is also left out of its model's ceiling choice (§3): the other hosts' checks choose the ceiling, the choice names the left-out host with the reason, and the model is excluded when no host remains. A cell in which any response's reported identifier differs from the expected one is labelled model_mismatch: it publishes on the route's page with the expected identifier, every distinct reported identifier and the number of requests that reported another, is excluded from pooled figures, does not count toward frame completeness (§4), and is re-run.
- **Response shape and ceiling, checked before characterization.** Before a route's first run, its ceiling check sends ten requests at the configured ceiling, at least 4 s apart, and records the response shape: the reported model identifier, which field of the streamed delta carries generated content, which field carries reasoning, where usage objects and any refusal field appear, and which finish-reason values it returns. The check passes when every request returned visible content with a finish reason other than the provider's length value and every one of the ten requests reported a prompt token count of at most the 1,200-token input bound; a target that reports none cannot establish the input bound and is not characterized. §3 gives the rule that chooses 512 or 1,024 for a model from the checks of every host of that model, less any host the reported-identifier rule above leaves out. A content predicate tuned to one field scores a model that carries its output in another as empty_stream on every request. A provider-model whose content field the §6 predicate does not recognize is not characterized until a numbered amendment (§0), adopted before that provider's first measured run, extends the predicate to it, and the recorded shape publishes with the run. A model that emits reasoning on a separate field is characterized: the reasoning is recorded apart and never counts as the answer (§6).
- **Routing products excluded.** An endpoint documented to dispatch across several models or several providers carries no single pinned identifier and is not measured under this document.
- **Endpoint and account.** Recorded per cell: the complete request URL, the provider's declared region or point of presence, taken from the endpoint configuration or a response header where the provider exposes one and never inferred from latency or addressing, the account tier, and the provider's published status for the endpoint at the cell's start and at its end. Routing behind the URL is the provider's and is not controlled; the resolved peer address is recorded per request (§9).
- **Terms review.** Before a provider's first run, the provider's terms applicable to the account are reviewed for provisions on automated access, on publication of measurement results, on republishing response metadata such as request identifiers, and on keeping the model's output, and the review is recorded with URL and retrieval date. A provider whose terms do not permit the planned traffic and publication is not characterized. Where the terms restrict republishing response metadata, the request identifier moves from the published JSONL artifact to a retained unpublished artifact. The route's configuration records what the terms say about keeping model output and the retention setting the route runs under (§9).
- **Account.** One account, one organisation and one key per provider, serving no other workload during the window, so recorded tier limits and 429 responses reflect this harness's traffic only; the run manifest records this. Each route's account type (a company's or an individual's ordinary paid account) and tier publish as found in the account's settings; the entity name is retained in the unpublished manifest copy until the operator's dated record publishes it; the account's creation date and its traffic before the window (probes and warm-ups, from the ledger) publish.
- **Documentation snapshots.** Every documentation record in this protocol (model eligibility, terms, tier limits, endpoint status, incident history) carries a retained copy of the retrieved page and its SHA-256 content hash, alongside the URL and retrieval date. A documentation change, wherever this document tests for one, is a difference between the text this protocol records from a page at a later retrieval and the same text in the retained copy. The limits and model documentation pages are re-read at each cell's start (§4, drift). On a route with a confidential limit, the cell manifests and the window-close record name each copy by its keyed hash (§9) in place of its SHA-256 hash.
- **Client host.** All 12 cells (§4) run from one dedicated non-spot virtual machine whose vendor is none of the measured providers, named with provider, region, size, image, operating system and kernel, with round-trip time (RTT) measured per cell per SPEC.md §6. Its open-file limit is recorded and is at least 65,536; an `EMFILE` error (no file descriptor left) makes the cell client-invalid. The run manifest records whether the client instance's cloud vendor is the measured provider or an affiliate of it. A characterization whose cells did not share one client host does not publish, because the grid would then measure the client alongside the endpoint.
- **Credentials.** The configuration carries only the environment-variable name (`target.api_key_env`). The key value is resolved at run start, travels in the header recorded for the route (§3), and is written to neither the configuration nor the run manifest.
- **One target per run.** A run addresses one endpoint and one model. Runs are never merged across endpoints, models, or account tiers.

## 3. Load profile (pinned)

A cell climbs five steps of offered load with one fixed request shape, and a step runs only where the ceiling rule admits it. The table summarises the pins; the bullets and paragraphs below are the rule.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Offered load | a ladder of 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 requests per second, ascending, Poisson arrivals (random, independent send times), open loop |
| Duration | 60 s warm-up at the first step, then 480 s per step with a 90 s drain after each |
| Expected requests | 120, 240, 480, 960 and 1,920 per step; 3,720 measured per cell; 44,640 per frame |
| Input | Shape S: the unique prefix, a space and a synthetic ticket, under a 1,200-token bound |
| Output ceiling | 512 tokens, or the pinned fallback of 1,024, chosen by the ceiling check across every host of the model less any host left out under §2 |
| Timeouts | a 90 s hard timeout from actual dispatch; a 30 s deadline from intended dispatch splitting completions into in time and late |
| Retries | zero |
| Request body | literal JSON per route, frozen by hash, with one placeholder for the user text; usage requested on chat-completions targets |
| Credential header | one header the provider documents for its key: `Authorization: Bearer`, `x-api-key` or `api-key` |
| Transport | Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) version 1.1; an idle pool of 752 connections |
| Admission | the ceiling rule: nominal demand at most half of every recorded rate, token, concurrency, daily and spend-window limit, with cover tests for the caps and for a limit documented without a value |
| Balance | read before each cell; a cell starts only where the balance covers its reservation, or is unavailable under the operator's dated ruling |

- **Ladder: Poisson arrivals at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 requests per second, ascending, open loop.** Each step is a 480 s measured window, after a 60 s warm-up at the first step's rate (15 requests expected, discarded). After every step comes a 90 s drain, as long as the hard timeout, in which nothing is sent, so every request of the step has ended before the next step starts. At each drain's end the step's status_429 fraction and G2 (§7) are judged: a status_429 fraction above 0.5 over the step's scheduled requests ends the ladder, with higher steps recorded as not attempted and the terminating step named; statuses the provider documents as overload stay in status_other (§5) and do not count toward the fraction. The instrument never retries and never resumes above a terminated step within the cell. "Under load" in any published sentence refers to this ladder and names the highest attempted step. One ladder is one cell (§4). The rate does not rise with the account tier: a higher tier supplies headroom, and the offered load stays identical so routes compare.
- **Expected sample budget: 120, 240, 480, 960 and 1,920 scheduled requests per step, 3,720 per cell and 44,640 per frame.** Arrivals are Poisson, so the realized count is a random variable around that expectation and is published per step and per cell. A request that opens a new connection inside a measured window carries its DNS (Domain Name System) lookup, connect and TLS (Transport Layer Security) handshake time in its client-observed TTFT. There is no fault instant and no cooldown phase. The generator drains every step to the 90 s hard timeout, so censoring inside the horizon is administrative (SPEC.md §3). Tail claims follow SPEC.md §7's policy against the realized completed count: a 95th or 99th percentile publishes only with its order-statistic interval, where both ranks fall inside the completed sample.
- **Shape S.** The user text is the per-request prefix `cs-<seed>-<index>` (six-digit index), a space, and a synthetic support ticket from the instrument's deterministic generator; the system text is frozen by hash. Input bound 1,200 tokens: every request of the ceiling check must report a prompt token count, none may exceed the bound, and the ticket byte cap is lowered before the freeze if one does. Output ceiling 512 tokens, with one pinned fallback of 1,024: each route's ceiling check sends ten requests at 512; where all ten return visible content with a finish reason other than the provider's length value on every host of the model not left out under §2, that model runs at 512; otherwise every such host runs at 1,024 after ten check requests there; a model that fails the same check at 1,024 on any such host is excluded, and the exclusion publishes. Deadline 30 s from intended dispatch; a later completion is late. Hard timeout 90 s from actual dispatch: a request with no terminal event by then is censored with its observation time. Requests run to their stop token; no output-length forcing is sent. Adding a shape is a numbered amendment.
- **Unique per-request prefixes**, per SPEC.md §6. Provider-side prefix-cache state is not verifiable (§6).
- **Distinct seed per cell.** `run.seed` is derived per cell with the dates (§4), so no two cells of a characterization repeat a request body.
- **Request body and credential, per target.** The complete request body is literal JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) in the configuration, frozen by its SHA-256 (256-bit Secure Hash Algorithm) hash; the harness replaces one placeholder with the request's user text and changes nothing else. Chat-completions targets: `model` (the deployment name on Azure), `messages` holding one system text and one user text, `stream: true`, `stream_options: {"include_usage": true}`, the output ceiling under the key the provider documents, or, where its documentation names neither, the one the route's ceiling check sent without a refusal (`max_completion_tokens` or `max_tokens`, recorded per target), and `reasoning_effort: "low"`; Groq additionally `reasoning_format: "parsed"`. Messages targets: `model`, `system`, `messages` holding one user text, `stream: true`, `max_tokens` at the ceiling, `thinking: {"type": "between_tools"}` and `output_config: {"effort": "low"}`, sent with the header `anthropic-version: 2023-06-01`. Temperature, top-p and every other sampling control are absent, so each provider's defaults apply; they are recorded where the provider publishes them and reported as not verifiable where it does not. Every host of one model carries the same reasoning value. A parameter a target rejects is recorded as not applied and publishes with the run. The credential travels in one header the provider documents for its key, recorded per target: `Authorization: Bearer`, `x-api-key` or `api-key`.
- **Client: 90 s HTTP hard timeout and 30 s deadline, zero retries, monotonic clock.**
- **Transport pinned to HTTP/1.1**, so one in-flight request occupies one connection and no two requests share stream-level flow control. The transport setting and a per-request record of the negotiated protocol are in the instrument (§9). `load.connections` is pinned at 32; the client adds it to the rate-derived provision of 720 (two times the top step's 4 requests per second times the 90 s hard timeout), so the idle pool holds 752 connections; concurrent connections are never capped.
- **Nominal demand per step:** the step's rate in requests per minute, and 1,200 input tokens plus the output ceiling per request, counted under each provider's documented rule (ceiling rule). Nominal, because the realized prompt token count depends on the provider's tokenizer (§6), and because realized Poisson arrivals can exceed the nominal rate inside any one minute or rolling window.

**Ceiling rule.** Before the schedule is frozen, every limit the provider publishes for the account, model and tier, every value the account console or a limits API shows, and every limit the operator set (a deployment's assigned tokens per minute, a monthly budget, a spend limit, a project spend cap, an auto-reload threshold) is recorded with URL or screenshot, retrieval date, retained copy and SHA-256 hash, or for a confidential limit its keyed hash (§9). A step is admitted only where its nominal demand is at most half of each: requests per minute and, where stated, per second; input, output and combined tokens per minute; concurrency, taken as the step's rate times the 90 s hard timeout; daily request and token quotas, taken as six cells, one per slot, at the largest cell's count, which bounds the route's scheduled cells on any Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) day; and rolling spend windows at the worst-case price per request. Nominal tokens per request are the 1,200-token input bound plus the output ceiling, counted under each provider's documented rule as a worst case: where a provider deducts input plus `max_tokens` at admission, that sum; where output tokens burn quota at a model-specific rate and the model is unlisted, the highest rate the provider documents for any model from the same developer. Monthly caps and the release's per-route liability caps must cover the route's worst case over its twelve scheduled cells, its two permitted re-runs and a probe allowance of 100 requests; a top step whose worst case would cross one is excluded. A prepaid balance must cover one cell: the auto-reload threshold is at least the worst case of the route's costliest cell. A dimension the provider documents but neither publishes a value for nor shows in the console caps the route at 0.5 requests per second or excludes it, by the operator's dated record before the freeze; a dimension the provider does not document imposes no test, and the absence publishes. Every exclusion publishes with the binding limit named. Admitted steps are fixed at the freeze. Each recorded limit is marked public or confidential (§9, confidential limits); an exclusion by a confidential limit names it by its dimension, source and keyed hash, without its value.

**Balance before each cell.** At each cell's start and end the route's balance command records the account's balance or quota, where the provider exposes one: the command exits 0 and prints one JSON object, `{"amount_usd": "<decimal>"}` or `{"unavailable": "<reason>"}`, and nothing else. A cell starts only where the amount is at least the cell's reservation (one probe request plus every scheduled request of the cell at the ledger's price), or where the balance is unavailable and the route's configuration carries the operator's dated ruling on why (`target.balance_unavailable`: its kind, `prepaid` or `postpaid`, the reason, the date and where the ruling is kept). A prepaid ruling needs a recorded auto-reload threshold (`auto_reload_threshold_usd`) and a postpaid one a recorded monthly spend limit (`spend_per_month_usd`), and the ceiling rule tests that limit at the route's start. A command that fails or prints anything else, an amount below the reservation, or an unavailable balance with no ruling blocks the cell before admission: no ledger line is written and no request is sent, and the cell is retained and labelled (§5). The reservation is also charged against the route's and the release's liability caps in the admission ledger (§9), and a refusal there stops the route.

**Schema note.** The configuration schema carries four profiles. `experiment` pins the SPEC.md §1 phase durations and a two-replica topology, and `ac` pins the SPEC.md §8 reference rate of 20 requests per second; both require a positive fault-window duration and a replica count that a managed endpoint does not expose, the full SPEC.md §6 pin list, which §6 of this document lists as not verifiable, and the SPEC.md §4 SLO block, which §7 of this document does not apply to hosted data. `pilot` carries the numbers of the drafted HA2 pilot, and `release` carries the ladder, shape and caps above; each refuses a value outside its pins. No profile expresses the v0.1 single-rate run (§0, superseded values).

## 4. Temporal sampling frame

A route runs twelve cells, one per slot and day type, on dates drawn from one master seed and committed before the window opens. The table summarises the frame; the bullets below are the rule.

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Slots | 00:00, 04:00, 08:00, 12:00, 16:00 and 20:00 UTC |
| Day types | weekday (Monday to Friday) and weekend (Saturday or Sunday); the six weekend cells include at least one Saturday and one Sunday |
| Cells per route | 12, one per slot and day type |
| Dates and seeds | drawn from one master seed by the fixed procedure below, committed before the window opens |
| Collection window | the dates committed with the schedule, at most 14 days, holding at least one weekday, one Saturday and one Sunday |
| Offsets | each route starts at its own pinned offset inside the slot: distinct multiples of 10 minutes drawn from the master seed |
| Start tolerance | 15 minutes, applied to the route's offset start |
| Re-runs | a cell is attempted at most twice; a route takes at most two re-runs in the window |
| Concurrent runs | permitted from one client host; listed in each manifest with the aggregate dispatch series |
| Drift | the limits and model documentation are re-read at each cell's start; a change inside the window splits the pooled figures and names the change; limits that no longer admit a step the route runs stop the route; after the last cell they are read once more as the window-close record |
| Completeness | fewer than 12 counted cells publish as labelled cells with no pooled figure |

- **Grid: 12 cells.** Six start slots in UTC, at 00:00, 04:00, 08:00, 12:00, 16:00, and 20:00, each executed twice: once on a weekday (Monday to Friday) and once on a weekend day (Saturday or Sunday). Day type is the UTC calendar day of the slot start, and the six weekend draws must include at least one Saturday and at least one Sunday. One ladder per cell, so 12 cells and an expected 44,640 measured requests per route when every step is admitted.
- **Dates and order fixed in advance.** The 12 date-and-slot pairs, their execution order, and the per-cell seeds are generated by a deterministic procedure from one master seed: eligible dates for each cell are enumerated in calendar order over the window, and each cell's date draw takes the SHA-256 of the master seed concatenated with the cell's label, reduced modulo that cell's eligible-date count; each cell's run seed for attempt 1 or 2 (§4 Re-runs) is the SHA-256 of the master seed concatenated with the cell's label, the attempt number and a fixed suffix, whose first 8 bytes read as a big-endian unsigned integer with the top bit cleared form `run.seed`. Where the resulting weekend dates violate the Saturday-and-Sunday rule above, an incrementing counter is appended to the master seed and the full schedule re-derived until they satisfy it, and the final counter value is committed alongside the master seed. The master seed and the resulting schedule are committed to this repository before the collection window opens. Changing a pair after the window opens is an amendment (§0).
- **Offsets.** Each route starts every cell at a pinned offset inside the slot, a permutation of distinct offsets at 10-minute spacing drawn from the master seed as part of the cell definition, so no two routes of a release share one.
- **Start tolerance: 15 minutes.** A run that starts more than 15 minutes after its slot plus the route's offset is not that cell.
- **Collection window: the dates committed with the schedule, at most 14 days.** All 12 cells complete inside that window, whose first and last dates publish with the characterization; the window holds at least one weekday, one Saturday and one Sunday.
- **Concurrent runs.** Runs of several routes from one client host are permitted and listed in each manifest, with the host's aggregate dispatch series over the cell.
- **Completeness.** A characterization with fewer than 12 counted cells does not publish as a characterization. Its cells publish as labelled cells, with no pooled figures.
- **Re-runs.** A cell whose run does not count toward frame completeness, for any reason in §2, §4 (start tolerance), §5, §7, or §9 is re-run at the next occurrence of the same cell inside the collection window. A re-run under this rule is not a change to a date-and-slot pair; the failed pair stays recorded in the manifest. A cell is attempted at most twice inside the window, and a route takes at most two re-runs inside the window; a cell that fails twice, or a route out of re-runs, leaves the frame incomplete. Both the failed attempt with its reason and the completed attempt are listed in the run manifest.
- **Drift inside the window.** The account tier's published limits and the model documentation are re-recorded at each cell's start by the route's refresh command, which rewrites the files of the operator file's limits and eligibility records and prints, for each recorded limit, the value it read or why it could not read one; a cell whose records were not rewritten after the command started, or whose command did not print that list, is blocked before admission (§5). A change to the limits or the documentation during the window, as defined under §2 documentation snapshots, splits the pooled figures: the cell at which the change was first observed and every later cell pool apart from the cells before it, and the characterization names the change. A change to a route's tier, limits or model documentation inside the window changes no admitted step. At each cell's start the ceiling rule (§3) is applied again with the values read in place of the recorded ones, a limit the command could not read keeping its recorded value, at the newest price recorded for the route in the admission ledger; where it no longer admits a step the route runs, the route stops before admission (§5), and the stop names the lowest such step and its binding limit. After the route's last cell the refresh command runs once more, and the limits and documentation read then are the route's window-close record. A route whose process stopped takes the record when its process next runs with no cell left. The record is compared with the last counted cell, and a change found there is named on the route's page and splits nothing, because no cell follows it. The provider's published incident history for the endpoint over the window is recorded by the operator beside the route's records.
- **Incidents.** A cell collected while the provider published an incident for the endpoint is labelled with that status and stays in the frame.
- **Reporting.** Pooled figures publish with the per-cell table beside them.
- **Grid limits.** It is a UTC grid: it does not equalize local business hours across providers, and no claim about a provider's peak or quiet period is made from it. A cell's figures describe that dated run and carry no run-to-run variance estimate.

## 5. Outcome model and the treatment of 429

Every sent request ends completed, errored or censored, and a scheduled request a stop left unsent is counted apart. The table summarises the lines; the paragraphs below are the rule.

| Outcome | Meaning | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| completed | the stream reached its terminator with at least one content event; in time when the whole answer arrived within the 30 s deadline from intended dispatch, late otherwise | the completed lines; only completions enter the latency figures |
| status_429 | an HTTP 429 response; never retried; `Retry-After` recorded and never honoured | its own line, a headline for the step |
| status_other | any other non-200 status, 503 and 529 included, with the provider's documented meaning annotated | one line per status code |
| connect | a failure before any status arrived, in DNS, the dial or the TLS handshake | its own line, attributed to neither side |
| reset | the connection was reset | its own line |
| empty_stream | HTTP 200 and the terminator, with no content event | its own line, split by finish reason |
| malformed_stream | a stream cut before its terminator, an undecodable event, or an in-band error object | its own line, with a sub-line per in-band error type |
| censored | no terminal event by the 90 s hard timeout from actual dispatch | the censored rate; a censored observation on the curve |
| unsent | a scheduled request of an attempted step that a stop left unsent | its own line, outside the three states; a censored observation at the horizon on the curve |

The SPEC.md §3 three-state outcome model applies to every request sent: it ends completed, errored, or censored, only completions enter latency histograms, and failure rates are first-class per step. The six SPEC.md §3 error classes apply unchanged, assigned by failure stage and status code. A scheduled request of an attempted step that a stop (below) left unsent is counted on the step's `unsent` line, outside the three states; the steps after a ladder end are recorded as not attempted, and their requests are never sent and carry no line.

**HTTP 429: errored, class status_429, reported on its own line.** A delivered 429 terminates the request as errored in the SPEC.md §3 class status_429, assigned by status code; no cause is inferred from the code, the headers, or the provider's documentation. The status_429 count and rate publish per step and per cell beside the SPEC.md §3 error rate, in every denominator. A `Retry-After` value and every response header the provider documents as a rate-limit or quota header are recorded per request (§9) and change no class; a release configuration must record `Retry-After`. `Retry-After` is never honored: retries are pinned at zero (SPEC.md §2), and honoring a back-off would convert the open-loop schedule into a closed loop.

**A nonzero status_429 fraction is a headline for its step**, published with the recorded limits of §3 and the step's nominal demand as a fraction of each public limit and, for a confidential one, the result of its test (§9). Poisson arrivals at half a recorded limit can still trip a limiter with no burst allowance, and a 429 can come from a limit dimension the ceiling rule does not check or from provider capacity, so the figure publishes as observed 429s at the recorded tier and offered load, with no claim about which limit or which cause produced them. A status_429 fraction above 0.5 at a step's drain ends the ladder (§3).

**Other delivered statuses.** Every other non-200 status is class status_other with its status code recorded, whatever the provider's documentation calls it: a 503, 529, or 498 that the provider documents as overload or capacity stays in status_other and is visible through the per-status counts of §8. Where the provider's published documentation names a status code as rate limiting, quota exhaustion, or capacity shedding, that mapping is recorded per run with URL and retrieval date and publishes as an annotation beside the per-status counts; it changes no class. Redirects are not followed (SPEC.md §3), so a 3xx is a delivered status in status_other.

**Streams.** A delivered reset is reset. The terminator is `data: [DONE]` for chat-completions targets and `message_stop` for Messages targets; a stream that ends before its terminator is malformed_stream, or reset where the transport reports one, per SPEC.md §3. A clean end before the terminator is `malformed_stream`. An in-band error object, before or after content, is `malformed_stream` with the object retained and a sub-line per documented error type; the first in-band error object ends the request at its arrival, and nothing after it is read. `empty_stream` publishes split by finish reason; completions at the length value publish on their own line; a terminator with no finish reason is completed with unknown termination noted. The harness never infers completion from stream end; where a provider documents a different stream terminator, the route does not publish until the client recognizes it.

**Blocks, ladder ends and stops.** Before a cell's ladder, from the client host, the cell's probe request (DNS resolution, TLS handshake and one request at the cell's settings) gates the start: a 400, 401, 403 or 404 on the probe, or an operator-set limit shown reached, blocks the cell's start. A cell whose limits and model documentation were not refreshed at its start (§4), or whose balance check does not let it start (§3), is blocked the same way, before admission. Inside a cell, a status_429 fraction above 0.5 at a step's drain ends the ladder, with higher steps recorded as not attempted, and the cell counts. A G2 failure, a tripwire (a dispatch beyond the schedule, or requests in flight above two times the step's rate times the hard timeout) or an `EMFILE` error ends the ladder and makes the cell client-invalid, so it is re-run. A release ledger refusal, a response the provider documents as its spend-limit or exhausted-balance refusal, a clock offset above 60 s, limits read at a cell's start that no longer admit a step the route runs (§4), or a 401 or 403 on a cell's probe that the route's previous probed cell also received, on its probe or inside its ladder, which the protocol takes as evidence of a credential or account fault, stops the route's process; the cell is retained, labelled with the reason, and re-run within §4's bound. Any other 401 or 403 inside a cell is status_other, and the cell stays in the record. Inside a cell every 4xx other than 429 is status_other, counted and published with its status code. A cell in which no request completes publishes with hosted-G6 marked failed and its outcomes split by class (§8). A cell in which a response reported an identifier other than the expected one is labelled model_mismatch and re-run (§2), unless a stop labels it stopped, with the mismatch among its reasons. An interrupted window is retained and labelled.

**Failures before a status.** A dial, DNS, or TLS failure before any response is errored under SPEC.md §3 in class connect, or reset where errors.Is(err, syscall.ECONNRESET) holds, with the Go error string recorded per request. It is a competing terminal event on the §8 incidence curve, counts toward the §8 conditional-percentile trigger, and stays in the error rate; the connect and reset counts publish on their own lines, attributed to neither side, because a refused dial or a failed handshake at the provider's edge is not distinguishable by the client from a fault on its own path. A cell whose connect fraction exceeds 1 percent publishes with that fraction as a labelled headline and with that cell's loopback canary result (SPEC.md §2: one stream from an in-process mock with fixed timing, read through the client's own read path) beside it; the cell is withheld only where G2 (§7) fails.

**Censoring.** No terminal event by the 90 s hard timeout, measured from actual dispatch, is censored at that timeout, with its observation time, per SPEC.md §3. A completion whose whole answer arrived more than 30 s after intended dispatch is late and stays completed.

The per-request HTTP status code is recorded in the run artifact (§9).

## 6. Pins: applied, and not verifiable

The client applies its own pins; the provider's cannot be verified and publish as not verifiable. The table summarises the three groups; the paragraphs below are the rule.

| Group | Pins |
|---|---|
| Applied unchanged, all client-side | the 512- or 1,024-token output ceiling, the 1,200-token input bound, the literal body with unique prefixes, open-loop arrivals, the 90 s hard timeout and the 30 s deadline, zero retries, the monotonic clock, the client-validity gate, the histogram configuration, the three-state outcome model |
| Added by this document | the ladder's rates and step duration, the warm-up and drain durations, the transport and connection figure, the per-cell seeds, the grid and its tolerances, the collection window, the client host, the ceiling rule, the balance check, the treatment of 429 and the probe rule |
| Not applicable or not verifiable | output-length forcing, the weights and revision behind the identifier, the key-value (KV) cache, prefix caching, batching, the serving stack and image, the scheduler, Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) graphs, the graphics processing unit (GPU) and driver fingerprints, the cluster network pins, per-replica counters |

This section restates SPEC.md §6's hosted-target lists and adds this document's own pins. Unverifiable items publish as unverifiable.

**Applied unchanged, all client-side:** the output ceiling of 512 or 1,024 tokens; the 1,200-token input bound; the literal request body with unique per-request prefixes; open-loop arrivals at each step's rate; the 90 s hard timeout and the 30 s deadline; zero retries; monotonic clock; the SPEC.md §2 client-validity gate; the single pinned HdrHistogram configuration (SPEC.md §3); the SPEC.md §3 three-state outcome model and estimator. Recorded per cell: the complete request URL, requested model name, client placement, RTT.

**Added by this document, and pinned here:** the ladder's rates and arrival process, the warm-up, step and drain durations, the transport protocol and connection figure, the per-cell seeds, the sampling grid and its tolerances, the collection window, the client host, the ceiling rule, the balance check, and the treatment of 429 and the probe rule of §5.

**Not applicable or not verifiable against a hosted endpoint:** ignore_eos and any output-length forcing; the model weights and revision behind the identifier; KV cache budget; prefix-caching state; continuous-batching verification; serving-stack version and image digest; scheduler settings; CUDA graph enablement; GPU SKU (stock-keeping unit), driver, and clock and power fingerprints; the Kubernetes, CNI (Container Network Interface), dataplane, kube-proxy, and node-monitor-grace-period pins; per-replica request counters.

**Tokenizer and usage.** The realized prompt token count and the realized completion token count are the provider's, produced by a tokenizer this protocol cannot pin or inspect. Usage is requested on chat-completions targets through `stream_options.include_usage` and arrives unrequested on Messages targets in `message_start` and `message_delta`; the ceiling check records where each target places it. Provider-reported prompt, completion and reasoning token counts are recorded per request and published as distributions; a target that returns no completion or reasoning count publishes those counts as not verifiable. A count the stream does not carry is recorded as absent, and an explicit 0 is a count. The visible answer is the content field; reasoning fields are recorded apart and never enter TTFT.

**Content events.** An SSE frame counts as a content event only where its delta carries a non-empty content string at the field the route's recorded response shape names; on a Messages target, a `text_delta` is content and a `thinking_delta` is reasoning. The content-event predicate carries the per-target response shape recorded at the ceiling check, including any refusal field, whose presence and length are recorded per request. A stream that reaches its terminator with no prior content event is errored, class empty_stream; a stream that ends any other way with no content event is reset or malformed_stream (SPEC.md §3). TTFT is timestamped at the first content event, so it is time to first generated token only under that predicate.

**Receive path.** SPEC.md §2's receive-path caveat applies in full and is aggravated here: client timestamps are taken when the read loop observes a frame, the network path is longer than a same-zone cluster path, and neither the provider's own TTFT histograms nor its event-coalescing behaviour is observable. The SPEC.md §2 loopback canary runs in every cell and bounds the client host's own read-loop lag per step; the margin between client-observed and provider-side timing stays unmeasured, and every hosted timing is labelled client-observed.

## 7. Run validity and the reported criterion

G2 can withhold a cell because the client machine was the bottleneck; hosted-G6 is only reported.

| Gate | What it tests | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| G2, client validity | send skew p99 at most 5 ms and max at most 50 ms; no scheduled request left undispatched; client CPU at most 70 percent over any 5 s window; Go garbage-collection pause p99 under 1 ms | judged per step at each drain's end; a failed step ends the ladder, the cell is client-invalid and is re-run |
| Hosted-G6, completion within the deadline | the fraction of scheduled requests completed within the 30 s deadline, against 0.99 | reported only, never run-invalidating |

**No SLO transfer.** The SPEC.md §4 SLO derives its end-to-end threshold from a forced 256-token budget (1000 ms plus 256 tokens at a 20 tokens-per-second floor, rounded up to 14 s), which hosted requests do not carry. Neither SPEC.md §4 threshold is applied to hosted data, and no hosted SLO is defined here. Latency publishes as distributions with no pass bar.

**Evaluable gates: G2 and hosted-G6.** Of the SPEC.md §10 gates, only these two are evaluable against a hosted target (SPEC.md §6). G1, G3, G4, G5, and G7 are reported as not applicable, never as passed.

- **G2, client validity, per step.** The SPEC.md §2 numbers unchanged: send skew (actual minus intended dispatch) p99 at most 5 ms and max at most 50 ms, zero scheduled-but-never-dispatched requests, client CPU (central processing unit) at most 70 percent sustained over any 5 s window, and Go garbage-collection pause p99 under 1 ms, each over the step's measured window and judged at the step's drain's end. A criterion the step could not measure does not pass. A G2 failure means the numbers describe the client, so the ladder ends there, the cell is client-invalid and does not count, and it is re-run under §4 from the lowest step.
- **Hosted-G6, completion within the deadline.** Hosted-G6 is the fraction of the step's scheduled requests whose whole answer arrived within the 30 s deadline from intended dispatch, over all scheduled requests of the step. A hosted cell has no pre-fault baseline window, so each step's measured window is its evaluation window. Hosted-G6's verdict is the point estimate against 0.99, the number SPEC.md §10 pins for G6, its baseline-goodput gate, carried over per SPEC.md §6; this document derives no hosted-specific threshold. It publishes per step and per cell with this definition attached and a 95 percent binomial (Wilson) interval at the realized denominator; the fraction over scheduled requests excluding connect and reset outcomes publishes beside it with its own interval, and the per-class error rates (§8) publish beside both. It is a property of this workload and deadline: completion within the deadline depends on realized output length and the provider's decode rate as well as on availability, and it publishes beside the completion-length distribution (§8).

**Hosted-G6 is reported, never run-invalidating.** A cell whose completion within the deadline falls below 0.99 publishes with the gate marked failed (SPEC.md §6).

## 8. Reporting

Every figure publishes per step with its denominator. Pooled rates and completion-incidence bands carry the design-effect adjustment below. Latency percentile intervals use request-level order statistics and assume independent completed requests.

| Figure | Over | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Completion within the deadline | scheduled requests | numerator and denominator, with its Wilson interval |
| Error rate by class, the censored rate and the unsent count | scheduled requests | one line per class, a count per HTTP status, a sub-line per in-band error type |
| Completion-incidence curve | scheduled requests | Aalen-Johansen point estimates (the share of scheduled requests completed by each time, with errors as competing events) with pointwise Wilson bands and the SPEC.md §3 quantile refusals inside the 90 s horizon |
| TTFT and end-to-end latency | completions only | distributions labelled conditional on completion, split by connection reuse |
| Completion length | completions, and the errored and censored requests apart | content-event counts, and provider-reported completion and reasoning tokens where the response carries them |
| Prompt length | requests sent | provider-reported prompt tokens where the response carries them |
| Inter-chunk latency | completions | pooled histograms of the gaps between content events |
| Time to the failure signal | errored requests, per class | the median and 90th percentile from intended dispatch |
| Throughput | the step's window | completions per second over the 480 s window; provider-reported completion tokens per second where completions carry the count |
| Nominal demand | each recorded limit | each step's nominal demand as a fraction of each public limit, and the result of the test for a confidential one, from the ceiling rule's verdict |

Per step and per cell, over the step's measured window:

- **Completion within the deadline** with its numerator and denominator, the SPEC.md §3 error rate with its split into the six SPEC.md §3 classes (status_429, status_other, reset, connect, empty_stream, malformed_stream), the censored rate, the unsent count, and the count per delivered HTTP status code, each on its own line over the same scheduled-request denominator, so completion, error, censored and unsent sum to one; the status_429 rate is observed under the pinned zero-retry client, and beside it print the step's highest nominal demand as a fraction of any public limit the ceiling rule tests at half, whether it is at most half of each confidential one, and the `Retry-After` record.
- **Aalen-Johansen completion-incidence curve** over all scheduled requests, with n and the SPEC.md §3 quantile-refusal rules inside the 90 s horizon, published as descriptive point estimates with pointwise bands. The curve does not decompose competing events by error class; the per-class rates above carry that split.
- **TTFT and end-to-end distributions**, completed-only, labelled conditional on completion: the median and 90th percentile, the 95th and 99th percentiles with their SPEC.md §7 order-statistic intervals where the ranks fall inside the completed sample, and the median and 90th percentile again by whether the connection was reused or newly dialled. A pooled distribution is computed over the completions of every cell it pools; its intervals assume independent completed requests, and the route page labels them so. Where the error-plus-censored fraction exceeds 5 percent, the SPEC.md §3 conditional-percentile rule applies and the incidence curve accompanies the percentiles.
- **Completion and prompt length as distributions**: the client-side content-event count per completed request, labelled as content events under the §6 predicate; the provider-reported completion and reasoning token counts where the response carries usage (§6); the content-event counts of the errored and of the censored requests as their own distributions; and the provider-reported prompt token count over the requests sent. Each publishes with how many requests carried the count, and as not verifiable where none did.
- **Time to the failure signal** per error class with events, the median and 90th percentile from intended dispatch, so a refusal that arrives in 50 ms and an overload signal that arrives after 25 s are distinguished.
- **Inter-chunk latency** as pooled per-step histograms of gaps between successive content events, labelled inter-chunk, never inter-token.
- **Throughput** as completions per second over the step's 480 s window, and the provider-reported completion tokens per second over the completions whose records carry the count.

**Intervals and bands.** Every per-cell, per-step rate publishes as numerator over scheduled requests with its 95 percent binomial (Wilson score) interval, labelled as treating the step's requests as independent. Pooled per-step figures across the frame's cells that attempted the step publish with the count, the range of the cell rates, the number of cells with at least one event, and a 95 percent Wilson interval on an effective sample size N / DEFF: for G cells, cell i with k_i events in n_i scheduled requests, pooled denominator N and pooled rate p, the design effect DEFF is the larger of 1 and [(G / (G − 1)) × Σ (k_i − p n_i)² / N²] / [p (1 − p) / N]; where fewer than two cells attempted the step, no cell has an event, or every request has it, DEFF is 1 and the interval is labelled as assuming independent requests. Each route's completion-incidence curve publishes with pointwise 95 percent Wilson bands per cell, and pooled with an effective sample size computed the same way at each time. Hosted-G6 is completion within the 30 s deadline over scheduled requests, per step and per cell, with its Wilson interval. No interval crosses routes.

**Pooled figures** over a complete frame of 12 counted cells publish per step with the per-cell table (§4); each step pools the cells that attempted it, over all of their scheduled requests, and describes those runs, estimating no average day and no provider-wide rate. Where the §4 drift rule splits the window, each side pools separately, labelled with its cell count and date range. No pooled figure is published for a route whose frame is incomplete.

**The index** across routes is the table §1 fixes: one row per route and step, alphabetical by provider, counts over the pooled scheduled count, the binding limit of an unadmitted step, and a link to the route's page. Where a route has no whole-frame pooled figures, its rows print in their place the number of its counted cells, for an incomplete frame, or the number of parts, for a frame the drift rule split; its page carries each cell's figures, or each part's pooled figures.

## 9. Required artifacts and the publication rule

A cell publishes only with every per-cell artifact below, and a release publishes only after the publication steps are recorded and checked. The table summarises the artifacts; the numbered list below is the rule.

| Artifact | Contents | Published |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument commit | the revision that produced the run, and the source diff of a modified build | yes |
| Configuration | the file as published: verbatim, with each confidential limit's value replaced by the quoted string `"[retained]"` | yes; the file as loaded is retained |
| Per-request record, `attempts.jsonl` | one JSON object per scheduled request: times, outcome, class, status, allowed headers, counts, the release fields | yes |
| Probe, clock, status, refresh and balance records | the cell's probe record; the clock-offset record; the endpoint status and balance records at the cell's start and end, and the refresh record at its start, with the limits it read; for a route with a confidential limit, the refresh command's output and that limit's value are withheld, as are the probe's non-200 bodies and in-band error text | yes; the withheld refresh record and probe texts are retained |
| Dispatch series | the cell's sends per UTC second, and the host's aggregate over the cell | yes |
| Ceiling-check records | the records of every host of the model read for the ceiling in force, with those of any host left out of the choice (§2), a confidential limit's snapshot without its value | yes, copied into each cell |
| Window-close record | per route: the refresh command's run after the route's last cell, the limits it read, and the operator records read after it, compared with the last counted cell | yes; the record copies are retained |
| Report pair | the metric set as JSON (`report.json`) and the text report (`report.txt`), from one configuration | yes |
| Run manifest | the run's records, every file by hash, the gates, and the cell record | yes |
| Raw response frames | each response body as read, up to the capture limits the manifest records, credentials redacted, and the values of the redacted headers | retained 12 months, unpublished, where the route's terms permit keeping model output; otherwise the 200 bodies are counted and not kept |
| Publication record | each route's notice, response, consent, permission and replication pack, every earlier disclosure and every pre-release copy, by date and hash | the dates and outcomes it records publish on the page; the file, which holds recipients and account identifiers, is retained with the entity record |

**Published per cell:**
1. The instrument commit that produced the run.
2. The configuration file as published: verbatim, except that each confidential limit's value is replaced by the quoted string `"[retained]"` (confidential limits, below).
3. The per-request JSONL artifact, one JSON object per request: index, intended dispatch time, actual dispatch time, first-token time, completion or failure time, outcome, error class, HTTP status code where a response arrived, `Retry-After` value where one was returned, every response header whose name the provider documents as a rate-limit or quota header verbatim on every response including refusals, the Go error string for a connect or reset outcome, content-event count, provider-reported usage with its completion and reasoning token counts where present, the resolved peer address, the connection-reuse flag, the provider's region or point-of-presence response header where one is emitted, the response's system-fingerprint field where present, the provider's request identifier where the response carries one (§2 terms review governs whether the identifier publishes or is retained unpublished), the finish reason, the first in-band error object, refusal presence and length where the shape names a refusal field, and the shape, step, ceiling, warm-up, in-time and late fields; no answer text. Rate-limit header values publish only where the provider's terms do not make the account's limits confidential, and are otherwise replaced by a stand-in in the published record and kept in the retained store. On a route with a confidential limit, the in-band error object publishes as a stand-in that keeps at most its `type` string, with the object's keyed hash: its HMAC-SHA256 (hash-based message authentication code over SHA-256) under the release key, a secret of 32 random bytes (confidential limits, below). The object is kept in the retained store. Per-request inter-chunk gaps stay out of the artifact; the report carries them as pooled histograms (SPEC.md §3).
4. The report pair: the full metric set as JSON and the human-readable report, from one configuration.
5. The run manifest: UTC start time, sampling cell, seed, provider, account tier, the complete request URL, requested and reported model identifiers, negotiated transport protocol, client host and RTT, the client host's wall-clock offset against its time-synchronization source at run start (where the recorded offset exceeds 60 s the route stops and the cell does not count), the provider's published endpoint status at start and end, the recorded tier limits (a confidential one without its value) and per-token prices with documentation URL and retrieval date, the account balance or quota at run start, with the check against the cell's reservation, and at run end, the §2 model-eligibility, dedicated-account, client-vendor-affiliation, and terms-review records as read at the cell's start, and the G2 and hosted-G6 results per step; and the cell record: the admitted and excluded steps with their binding limits, the ceiling in force, the check records that chose it and each host left out of the choice with the reason, the offset, the concurrent-run list, the aggregate dispatch series, the account age and pre-window volume, the naming record, and the cell's label with its reasons. The §2 documentation snapshots publish as content hashes in the manifest, keyed on a route with a confidential limit, with retained copies beside the response frames below.

**Retained, unpublished:** the raw response frames per request, credentials redacted, kept for 12 months from the characterization's publication date where the route's recorded terms permit keeping model output, and otherwise not kept, with each 200 body's bytes counted in their place; the values of the response headers a provider's terms keep confidential, from the attempts file and from the probe and ceiling-check records, and a limits-read body the terms keep unpublished; for a route with a confidential limit, the in-band error objects of the attempts file and the non-200 bodies and in-band error objects of the probe and ceiling-check records; limit snapshots a provider's terms protect; the configuration file as loaded, which holds each confidential limit's value, and the refresh records as run of a route with a confidential limit; the release key; the entity record.

**Confidential limits.** Every recorded limit carries a marking. A limit whose value a public documentation page gives is public, and its value publishes in full. A limit whose value only the account console shows, under terms that keep it confidential, is confidential: its value is retained. The configuration as published, the ceiling rule's verdict (each step's nominal demand and the result of its test, with no value and no fraction), the binding limit of an excluded step or of a route stop, and the ceiling-check records' limit snapshots name the limit by its dimension, its source and the keyed hash of its retained snapshot; the refresh records and the window-close record list it by its dimension, marked confidential, without its value. The manifest and the schedule name the configuration as published by its SHA-256 hash, and the schedule also names the file as loaded by its keyed hash. No published record carries an unkeyed hash of the file as loaded, from which the value could be recovered by trial. On a route with a confidential limit these published hashes are also keyed: those of the in-band error objects, in the attempts file and in the probe and ceiling-check records; those of the non-200 bodies and of a withheld limits body, in the probe and ceiling-check records; and a cell manifest's references to the frame store and to the operator records, which the window-close record also names by keyed hash. A keyed reference carries no byte count, and every other file a manifest names keeps its SHA-256 hash and size. The operator creates the release key once, before the release's first ceiling check, and keeps it out of this repository and every published file, as the credentials are. Without the key, a guessed value cannot be tested against a keyed hash. Each test's result and the nominal demand publish, so the published demands bound a confidential limit: the test's bound (half the limit, or the whole limit where the limit must cover the demand) is at or above every demand that passed and below every demand that failed. The configuration as published does not load as a run configuration, so a cell of a route with a confidential limit is replayed and checked from the retained file as loaded.

**Publication steps**, each recorded in the release publication record with its date. Each provider receives its own per-step tables and the evidence pack, with a factual-accuracy window of at least four working days, the account identifier so it can match the traffic, and a request for written consent to publish its response verbatim. Amazon Web Services (AWS) also receives the replication pack its Service Terms §1.8 requires, no later than its notice. The final page text follows. A response publishes verbatim only with the provider's written consent; otherwise the page records the date a response arrived and the corrections made. The page carries the corrections log, the naming rules, the account's ordinary paid status and the absence of provider funding, and lists every provider told of the measurement before the notices and how, from the record's entry for each email and form. A pre-release copy sent before publication is recorded with the routes whose figures it withheld, and withholds every route whose provider requires the replication pack. The record names the cell manifests and the pages by hash, and the release publishes only when the check of the record against the cells and the pages passes.

**Known limits.** The instrument does not prevent the following.
- A record is checked for agreement with itself, its configuration and the schedule, and nothing shows that it is genuine. Nothing ties a ceiling-check record to its lines in the ledger: a hand-built record whose fields agree passes, as does a check admitted in a copy of the release ledger.
- A ledger is known by the SHA-256 hash of its first line. A copy of the release ledger, or an earlier version of it restored at the same path, has that first line and passes every check, so spend recorded in another copy, or after the restored version, does not count against the caps.
- An endpoint whose requests need a query string (the part of a URL after `?`) cannot be configured: `target.endpoint_url` refuses one.
- On a route with a confidential limit, the stand-in for an in-band error object keeps the provider's `type` string, and the `Retry-After` and request-identifier headers publish as sent. A number the provider puts in any of them publishes. A withheld body's stand-in also shows whether the body was longer than the probe or the ceiling check reads.

**Required instrument changes.** These land separately from this document, and a run executed while one it needs is absent does not publish:
- the full-length acceptance run on the client host as an evidence file: the command exists (`percentes-release rehearse`), and the client host has not yet run it at full length;
- for the pilot: the report pair of a window and the paired report; a window's manifest names no pair, so the completeness check and the replay do not apply to it.

**Publication rule.** A cell publishes only with every per-cell artifact the table above marks as published and, where the route keeps model output, the retained frame store. A characterization publishes only with 12 counted cells inside one collection window; an incomplete frame's cells publish labelled incomplete, with no pooled figures. A release publishes only when the publication record's check passes.

## Appendix A. Amendment text as adopted

The adopted text of HA1 and HA3, whose adoption §0's log records. §1 to §9 carry it; where this appendix and a body section differ, the body section governs.

### HA1 as adopted

Drafted 24 August 2026, revised 26 September 2026. It replaced the single pinned rate and the headroom rule of v0.1 §3 with a ladder of rates and a ceiling rule. HA3 point 5 modifies it where the two differ: the drain, the backoff and G2 evaluation at each drain's end, and the ceiling rule, which point 6 replaces. HA1 numbers that HA3 changed are stale below, and §3 carries the changed values: concurrency is the step's rate times the 90 s hard timeout (HA1: the 30 s timeout); tokens per request are the 1,200-token input bound plus the output ceiling, with no doubling; the daily window is the UTC day (HA1: the provider's documented quota window); the idle pool is 752 connections (HA1: 272). Its ladder, backoff rule and per-step publication are in §3 and §8, and its client-validity rule in §7. The text as drafted:

**Ladder.** Offered load is a ladder of pinned steps, open-loop Poisson arrivals at each step: 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 requests per second, run in ascending order, each step an 8-minute measured window, after the §3 60 s warm-up run once at the first step. One ladder is one cell (§4).

**Ceiling rule, replacing headroom.** Before a ladder runs, every numeric limit the provider publishes for the (provider, account, model) triple is recorded with URL (Uniform Resource Locator) and retrieval date: request ceilings per minute and per day, token ceilings per minute and per day, concurrency caps, and spend caps with the per-token prices they are computed from. A step's nominal demand is its rate times the pinned request shape over the window a ceiling states, token demand doubled as the allowance for the provider's tokenizer (§6), and spend at the recorded prices. A step is excluded before the run where its demand over 60 s reaches a recorded per-minute ceiling, where its rate times the pinned 30 s timeout reaches a recorded concurrency cap, or where its demand over 8 minutes, added to every lower step and to every other ladder scheduled for the triple on the same day, reaches a recorded per-day or spend ceiling, the day being the provider's documented quota window or, where none is documented, the UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) day. The exclusion publishes alongside the results with the binding ceiling named. A provider publishing no ceilings has no excluded steps for that reason, and the absence is published. The §3 balance and quota records stay as they are.

**Backoff rule.** A step whose status_429 fraction (§5, the SPEC.md §3 class status_429) over the step's scheduled requests exceeds 0.5 terminates the ladder: higher steps are recorded as not attempted with the terminating step named. Statuses the provider documents as overload stay in status_other (§5) and do not count toward the fraction. The instrument never retries and never resumes above a terminated step within the cell.

**What publishes per step.** The §5 outcome split (completed, errored with class, censored), the status_429 fraction as a headline, the completion-incidence curve, and the conditional latency quantiles, each labelled with the step's offered rate. "Under load" in any published sentence refers to this ladder and names the highest attempted step.

**Client validity.** The SPEC.md §2 client-validity gate, G2 in §7, applies per step. A step failing it is withheld and the ladder stops there; a ladder stopped by a G2 failure does not count toward frame completeness, and its cell is re-run under §4 from the lowest step.

**Consequential edits on adoption.** §0: "under a fixed open-loop load" becomes "at each step of the pinned ladder"; §1: the question is asked at each ladder step. §3: the rate, duration (60 s warm-up, then five 8-minute measured windows), expected-sample-budget (rate times 480 s per step, 120 to 1,920 scheduled requests) and nominal-demand (per step) bullets are restated; in the transport bullet, `load.connections` 32 is added to the provision derived from the top step, two times 4 requests per second times 30 s, so an idle pool of 272; the headroom paragraph is superseded by the ceiling rule above. §4: a cell is one ladder; the expected request count is 3,720 scheduled requests per cell and 44,640 per characterization. §5: the status_429 headline publishes per step, and its paragraph's references to the headroom rule and to half the published rate are restated against the ceiling rule. §6: the ladder's rates and step duration join the pins this document adds, and "the headroom rule" in that list becomes "the ceiling rule". §7: G2 applies per step as above. §8: every per-run figure publishes per step, labelled with the rate, and pooled figures pool each step separately across the 12 cells. §9: the required hosted profile carries the ladder steps and one measured window per step in place of the single §3 rate, with the connection provision derived from the top step; the required instrument changes gain the stepped schedule with per-step windows, the pre-run ceiling exclusion, the termination rule, and per-step G2 evaluation and reporting.
